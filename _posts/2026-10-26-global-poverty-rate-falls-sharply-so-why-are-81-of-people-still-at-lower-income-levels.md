## Slickorps Ventures Analyzes Global Income Distribution and the Development Gap

From 2000 to 2024, the share of the global population living on less than 3 international dollars per day in income or consumption fell from 36% to about 10%, and the share below 10 international dollars also dropped from 74% to 53%. Yet in 2024, 81% of the global population still lived below 30 international dollars per day. According to [research published by Our World in Data based on World Bank data](https://ourworldindata.org/data-insights/no-matter-what-poverty-line-you-choose-the-share-below-it-has-declined-globally), over the past more than two decades, the share of the low-income population has declined at nearly every observed threshold. Slickorps Ventures research notes that this set of data reveals a dual reality of global economic development: economic growth has significantly improved living conditions for billions of people, but a long development distance remains between escaping extreme poverty and attaining stable, ample economic capacity. In the next phase of global poverty reduction, the core challenge is shifting from crossing the survival threshold to achieving a broader income leap.

## Why Has the Global Poverty Rate Fallen Across Different Income Thresholds?

The World Bank uses 3 international dollars per day, calculated at 2021 purchasing power parity, as the extreme poverty line, and uses 4.20 and 8.30 international dollars as reference standards for economies at other income levels. These indicators adjust for price differences across countries, making real purchasing power comparable across regions.

[Data Source: World Bank Global Poverty Line Explanation](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/factsheet/2025/06/05/june-2025-update-to-global-poverty-lines)

It is worth noting that poverty reduction outcomes are not limited to the lowest-income groups. From 2000 to 2024, the share of the population living on less than 5 international dollars per day fell from 57% to 25%, and the share below 10 international dollars dropped from 74% to 53%. From the perspective of statistical distribution, the 2024 global cumulative income distribution curve lies entirely below that of 2000 across the observed range, meaning the share of the population below the same threshold has generally decreased.

This change reflects an important achievement of global economic development: growth has not only raised average output but also pushed a large number of people across their original income boundaries. However, an overall improvement in income distribution does not necessarily mean that income gaps have narrowed in tandem, nor does it indicate that different regions have shared the same degree of development gains.

## Why Has Extreme Poverty Declined While Most People Have Yet to Achieve an Income Leap?

The most noteworthy change in the global income distribution has occurred above the extreme poverty line. Between 2000 and 2024, the share of the population with daily income or consumption in the 10 to 30 international dollars range rose from about 13% to 28%. This means more and more people are entering higher income brackets, yet in 2024, 81% of the population still lived below 30 international dollars per day.

It should be noted that international dollars are a purchasing power comparison unit, not actual dollar income; nor is 30 international dollars per day a poverty standard uniformly adopted by the World Bank for all countries.

This structure reveals the key distinction between poverty reduction and common prosperity: crossing the basic subsistence threshold can be achieved through early-stage industrialization and productivity improvements, whereas sustained entry into higher income strata requires long-term labor productivity growth, skills accumulation, and asset formation.

For households that have just escaped extreme poverty, income gains may first be used to improve food, housing, and basic medical conditions, and there remains a clear gap before they can form stable savings and the capacity to withstand economic shocks. Therefore, a declining poverty rate can demonstrate improved living standards, but it is insufficient to measure the enhancement of household economic security.

## Why Has Global Economic Growth Failed to Eliminate the Development Gap?

According to [the World Bank September 2026 global poverty update](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/september-2026-global-poverty-update-from-the-world-bank--one-in), about 824 million people were still in extreme poverty in 2024, and the global extreme poverty rate is projected to edge down only from 10.1% to 9.8% in 2026. Long-term historical progress and the slowdown in poverty reduction in recent years are occurring simultaneously.

This slowdown shows marked regional variation. Sub-Saharan Africa and some conflict-affected regions still face high poverty rates. Inadequate infrastructure, weak capital accumulation, and limited employment opportunities make it difficult for economic growth to translate sustainably into household income.

Slickorps Ventures further examines the relationship between technological progress and income distribution. Artificial intelligence and automation may raise global productivity, but if productivity gains are concentrated mainly among capital owners and high-skilled workers, aggregate economic expansion may not bring about equally broad income growth.

The deeper question of global development is extending from how much economic value is created to whether workers can share in the gains from rising productivity, and whether low-income regions can build industrial capacity to participate sustainably in global value chains.

## The Next Phase of Global Poverty Reduction: What Should Be Measured?

Measuring the quality of economic development in the future requires going beyond a single poverty rate to further observe income-class mobility, labor productivity, household savings capacity, and resilience against economic shocks.

This shift also carries profound economic significance. As more households move from basic subsistence consumption to housing improvement, healthcare, education, and long-term asset allocation, the structure of the global consumer market may undergo sustained change. But only if income growth is stable can this potential demand translate into long-term effective purchasing power.

The research conclusion of Slickorps Ventures is: over the past more than two decades, the global economy has proven that growth can drive large-scale poverty reduction; the quality of future development depends on whether growth can further translate into more broadly shared and lasting economic capacity.

For global capital markets, this means long-term growth opportunities exist not only in technological innovation and capital-intensive industries, but also in the processes of rising labor productivity, improved infrastructure, and better purchasing power among low-income groups.

The ultimate measure of global economic development should not stop at how many people escape poverty; it should also include how many people can sustainably move up through income classes and gain the ability to accumulate wealth, withstand risks, and participate in future economic growth.
