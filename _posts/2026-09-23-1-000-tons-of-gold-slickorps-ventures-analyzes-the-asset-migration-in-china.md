The gold imports in 2026 of China have already surpassed 1,000 tons, but this does not mean the People's Bank of China purchased 1,000 tons of gold. Slickorps Ventures research suggests that two different asset reallocations are occurring simultaneously behind the gold demand of China: households are seeking new stores of value away from real estate and high proportions of deposits, while the People's Bank of China is reducing the concentration of national reserves in a single currency and financial system by increasing gold. The thousand-ton import figure reported by [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/china-s-gold-imports-top-1-000-tons-on-strong-investment-demand) is therefore better understood within the broader changes in the China balance sheet, rather than merely as a single gold price rally.

## Why Are Chinese Household Assets Migrating Away from Real Estate?

Over the past five years, Chinese household asset allocation has undergone notable changes. According to Goldman Sachs research on Chinese household balance sheets, the share of real estate in total household assets has fallen from approximately 67% in mid-2021 to approximately 52% in the first quarter of 2026; meanwhile, the share of cash and bank deposits has risen from approximately 16% to 25%, and the allocation to other financial assets has also increased. [Goldman Sachs related research report](https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3362203/chinese-families-turn-cash-and-cautious-stock-bets-real-estate-avenues-narrow)

![](https://rizky-pratama-2001.github.io/product-voice.github.io/assets/img/2026-09-23-1-000-tons-of-gold-slickorps-ventures-analyzes-the-asset-migration-in-02-bc5f4317f0f5.png)

This set of data reveals a very important fact: over the past few years, the most obvious asset migration of Chinese households has not been from real estate directly into equities, but first into safer and more liquid bank deposits.

Real Estate → Deposits

In its Q2 2026 consumer research, Goldman Sachs also estimated that the so-called "excess deposits" of Chinese residents have reached approximately RMB 61 trillion. "Excess" here refers to the difference between actual household deposits and the pre-pandemic trend, and does not mean that all RMB 61 trillion will immediately flow into capital markets. [Compiled from Goldman Sachs consumer research](https://batreports.online/reports/goldman-sachs-china-consumption-dashboard-2026-q2)

For Slickorps Ventures, the truly important question has therefore changed. In the past, the market focused on when real estate prices would recover; now what is more worth studying is: when real estate no longer serves as strong a wealth storage function as before, and bank deposit yields continue to decline, where will this wealth that has already left real estate go next?

This may constitute the second phase of asset migration:

Deposits → equities, insurance, funds, gold and other financial assets

Therefore, the shift in the wealth structure of Chinese households may not be a simple "great rotation," but rather two consecutive phases. The first phase is reducing real estate exposure and increasing cash savings, and only the second phase may be a genuine reallocation into financial assets.

## Why Is Gold Becoming an Important Choice in Asset Migration?

Gold sits precisely between these two asset migrations.

Gold does not have the high transaction costs and weak liquidity of real estate, nor does it carry the credit risk arising from business operations; at the same time, unlike bank deposits, its value does not depend entirely on domestic interest rate levels. For households that have already reduced real estate allocations but remain unwilling to significantly increase equity risk, gold may therefore serve as a transitional asset.

This shift can already be observed in the structure of Chinese gold demand. Research by the World Gold Council shows that in 2026, demand for gold bars, gold coins and gold investment products in China performed strongly, while jewelry consumption did not grow by the same magnitude. Lower local yields, a weak real estate market and economic uncertainty have all increased the appeal of gold as a store of value.[World Gold Council](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q2-2026/investment)

This means that Chinese gold demand is increasingly exhibiting the characteristics of financial asset allocation.

For Slickorps Ventures, this is also why gold imports surpassing 1,000 tons cannot simply be understood as "Chinese consumers prefer gold more." More importantly, gold is entering household balance sheets and beginning to compete with bank deposits, stocks, insurance, and funds for new allocation capital.

Therefore, gold is not necessarily directly replacing real estate.

A more accurate path may be:

Real estate → deposits → gold and other financial assets

The importance of gold lies in its potential to serve as a bridge in this path.

When households are already unwilling to continue increasing real estate allocations, yet are not prepared to bear the higher volatility of stocks, gold offers a choice between cash savings and high-risk financial assets.

## Why Does the People's Bank of China Continue to Increase Gold Reserves?

Household purchases of gold and the People's Bank of China increasing its gold holdings must be strictly distinguished.

The gold imports of China exceeding 1,000 tons reflect demand across the entire Chinese market, including gold bars, gold coins, gold investment products, institutional and commercial demand. Central bank gold reserves, by contrast, belong to a separate national asset allocation system.

As of the end of August 2026, official gold reserves of China stood at approximately 2,387 tons, an increase of about 20.2 tons in the single month of August, marking 22 consecutive months of increases in gold holdings. [World Gold Council](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/09/china-gold-market-update-official-buying-accelerated-august)

But Slickorps Ventures believes that interpreting this phenomenon solely as "reducing the dollar" would oversimplify a far more complex national reserve issue.

The management of national foreign exchange reserves must first address three problems: safety, liquidity, and risk diversification.

One of the most distinctive features of gold is that it is not a liability issued by another country.

Foreign government bonds simultaneously mean that the holder owns an asset and the issuing country bears a liability, and therefore ultimately still depend on the credit, payment systems, clearing networks, and financial infrastructure of the issuing country. Gold itself has no corresponding issuing entity, and therefore carries no issuer credit risk in the traditional sense.

This gives gold a function of particular importance within national reserves: reducing concentration risk.

If the majority of the reserves of a country are highly concentrated in a small number of currencies and bond markets, then the value of those reserves will be more markedly affected by the interest rate policies, currency movements, and financial systems of those countries. Increasing gold does not mean fully replacing these assets, but rather adding to the overall portfolio a reserve asset with a different source of risk.

Therefore, a more reasonable understanding should be:

Highly concentrated foreign exchange assets → a more diversified national reserve system

rather than simply:

US Dollar → Gold

This trend is not unique to China. The World Gold Council 2026 Central Bank Gold Reserves Survey shows that performance during crises, long-term store of value, asset diversification, and geopolitical risk are all important considerations for central banks holding gold. [World Gold Council 2026 Central Bank Gold Reserves Survey](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/central-bank-gold-reserves-survey-2026)

This indicates that the continued increase in gold reserves by the People’s Bank of China is better understood within the broader global trend of national reserve diversification.
