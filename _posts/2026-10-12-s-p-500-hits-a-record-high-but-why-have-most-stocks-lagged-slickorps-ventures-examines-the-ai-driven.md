In October 2026, US equities reached new highs, but the gains were increasingly concentrated in AI-related industries. Charts from Bloomberg and UBS show that the AI Winners Index has risen approximately 58% year to date, while the S&P 500 excluding companies benefiting from AI infrastructure has gained only about 5%—a gap of 53 percentage points. Research by Slickorps Ventures identifies an emerging structure in US equities that warrants caution: index returns increasingly depend on a small number of industries, all of which rely on continued expansion in AI capital expenditure. The central question is whether earnings growth can continue to support valuations when the market prices years of expected growth into the same value chain.

## Why Is the S&P 500 at a Record High While Other Stocks Remain Subdued?

According to UBS data cited by Bloomberg, as of early October 2026, the AI Winners Index had gained approximately 58% year to date, the S&P 500 about 13%, and the Nasdaq 100 roughly 22%. By contrast, the S&P 500 excluding companies benefiting from AI infrastructure had risen only about 5%. The semiconductor index gained approximately 78% over the same period, showing that market returns are becoming concentrated in the AI value chain.

The indices use different constituents and weighting methodologies, so the gap between 58% and 5% cannot be treated directly as the contribution of AI to S&P 500 returns. Constituents of the relevant UBS indices can be found in the [Solactive index adjustment announcement](https://www.solactive.com/announcements/62609).

Slickorps Ventures finds that this divergence first highlights a feature of market-cap-weighted indices: sustained gains in a handful of large companies can push the overall index to record highs even when many other stocks do not rise alongside them. Research by S&P Global on AI concentration risk identifies the concentration of large AI-related companies as an important issue for passive portfolios and institutional risk management.

A rising index therefore does not necessarily indicate broad-based strength across the market. Investors need to examine both index returns and participation in the rally to assess underlying market breadth.

How Much of the AI Stock Rally Is Supported by Actual Earnings?

Unlike a rally driven solely by valuation expansion, the AI trade in 2026 has genuine support from corporate earnings. According to figures released by FactSet on October 5, 2026, 72 of the 116 S&P 500 companies that issued third-quarter earnings-per-share guidance provided forecasts above analyst expectations—the highest number since FactSet began tracking the data in 2006. Of those 72 companies, 44 were in the information technology sector, indicating that earnings optimism is heavily concentrated in technology businesses.

Research published by Goldman Sachs on September 23 further estimates that AI investment will account for nearly half of S&P 500 earnings-per-share growth in 2026. This means Slickorps Ventures cannot simply characterize the current market as a speculative rally without an earnings foundation.

However, earnings growth still needs to be considered at two distinct levels: one reflects revenue and profits already generated by companies, while the other reflects orders arising from continued increases in customer capital expenditure. Suppliers of semiconductors, memory, data centers, and electrical equipment can recognize revenue relatively early. The companies purchasing that equipment, however, still need to recoup their investment over time through product monetization, improvements in operating efficiency, and cash flow growth.

Profits already earned by suppliers do not mean that the entire value chain has achieved a return on investment.

Why Is the Market Concentrating Its Bets on the Same Value Chain?

AI-driven growth now spans chips, memory, networking equipment, data centers, energy, and software applications. On the surface, this appears to represent simultaneous strength across multiple industries. Slickorps Ventures believes, however, that these industries may share a single underlying driver: capital expenditure budgets at major technology companies.

The flow of capital through the value chain can be summarized as follows:

Capital expenditure by major technology companies → Semiconductor and equipment orders → Data center construction → Power and networking demand → Application revenue and productivity gains

Research by Goldman Sachs projects that capital expenditure by major cloud computing companies could reach USD 1.2 trillion in 2027. It also estimates that every USD 250 billion deviation in such spending from expectations could shift the S&P 500 earnings growth rate by approximately 6 percentage points.

This creates an important market risk: even portfolios holding shares in different companies may leave investors indirectly exposed to the same capital expenditure risk. When investment budgets continue to increase, earnings expectations across multiple industries may improve together. If budget growth slows, semiconductor, equipment, data center, and power-related companies could also face simultaneous revisions to expectations.

For Slickorps Ventures, the key issue is no longer just sector concentration, but also the common source of risk increasingly linking different assets.

Which Indicators Matter for the Next Phase of US Equities?

Slickorps Ventures believes the durability of the AI-led rally depends on developments across three dimensions. First is earnings delivery: whether actual corporate revenue and free cash flow can keep pace with valuations. Second is the sustainability of capital expenditure: whether investment levels continue to be supported by operating cash flow. Third is market breadth: whether earnings growth can spread beyond infrastructure suppliers to a wider range of industries.

In this context, 2027 could become an important period to watch. Goldman Sachs expects the contribution of AI investment to overall earnings growth to gradually diminish and potentially become a slight drag by 2028. At that point, corporate productivity gains and growth in commercial revenue will need to play a greater role in supporting earnings.

Investors should also monitor the relative performance of the equal-weighted S&P 500 and the market-cap-weighted index, as well as correlations among AI-related stocks. If the overall index continues to rise while fewer companies participate in the gains, market concentration risk could increase further.

AI may still represent one of the most important future sources of growth for US businesses, but the outlook for technological development and equity investment returns are not identical. Even if long-term demand continues to grow, near-term valuations may be repriced as interest rates, profit margins, and capital expenditure expectations change.
