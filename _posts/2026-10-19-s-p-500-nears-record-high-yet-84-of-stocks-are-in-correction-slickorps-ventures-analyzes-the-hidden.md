In October 2026, a marked divergence emerged in the US stock market. The S&P 500 stood only about 2% below its all-time high, yet 84% of its constituent stocks had fallen at least 10% from their respective record highs. Market statistics show that since the low of March 30, just 10 stocks contributed roughly 70% of the rebound gains of the index. **Slickorps Ventures research finds that this divergence reveals a disconnect between the index-level prosperity of US equities and the internal market structure: a handful of large technology companies continue to drive the index higher, while a large number of other stocks remain under pressure from financing costs, energy prices, and shifting earnings expectations.** The question worth watching now is whether this correction, masked by the index, signals accumulating risk or an opportunity for a new round of broad-based gains?

## Why Is the S&P 500 Near a Record High While Most Stocks Remain Weak?

The S&P 500 is calculated on a market-capitalization-weighted basis, giving large companies far greater influence over the index than other constituents. Market statistics from The Kobeissi Letter show that 84% of constituent stocks have drawn down at least 10% from their respective all-time highs, 59% have drawn down at least 20%, 41% have fallen more than 30%, and another 17% have drawn down more than 50%. These percentages are cumulative statistics reflecting adjustments relative to historical highs, and do not mean that the same proportion of stocks declined during 2026.

At the same time, large companies such as Nvidia, Micron, Apple, and Microsoft have supported the overall market through their higher index weightings and, in some cases, strong share price performance. Related research from Goldman Sachs notes that participation in the US equity rally has fallen to its lowest level since the dot-com bubble era, with the median stock within the index nearly 20% below its own all-time high.

**Slickorps Ventures describes this phenomenon as a classic structural "hidden bear market": the index remains strong, yet a large number of stocks undergo deep corrections.** It does not mean the entire market has entered a bear market, but it shows that index performance is no longer sufficient to fully reflect the risks within the market.

## Why Are US Equity Valuations Clearly Stratifying?

The unevenness of capital costs and earnings growth is an important cause of market divergence. Data from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics show that in September 2026 the Consumer Price Index rose 3.7% year-over-year, with energy prices up 20.4%. Inflationary pressure has led the market to reassess the interest rate outlook and has also increased the financing burden on some companies.

However, the capacity of different industries to withstand pressure varies. Large technology enterprises with abundant cash flow and rapid profit growth are more easily able to offset financing costs and valuation pressure through profit expansion; real estate, manufacturing and other capital-intensive enterprises are more susceptible to the impact of rising borrowing costs and energy expenditures.

Earnings data likewise require cautious interpretation. According to FactSet and related analysis, in the second quarter of 2026, S&P 500 earnings per share grew approximately 52.6% year-over-year, but this includes large valuation gains generated by equity holdings of certain technology enterprises. After excluding the relevant effects, overall earnings growth remains strong, yet lower than the headline figure.

**This means the market is re-distinguishing between accounting profits, sustainable operating earnings and real cash flow.** For investors, whether an enterprise can sustainably generate profits is more worthy of attention than the earnings growth rate of a single quarter.

## Widespread Deep Stock Corrections, Do They Signal New Investment Opportunities?

Broad-based adjustments within the market may also create conditions for the next rally. If energy prices decline, inflationary pressure eases and the financing environment improves, some previously suppressed industries may regain valuation support.

But Slickorps Ventures emphasizes that **a stock price decline does not equal investment value.** Declining profitability, rising debt or weakened long-term growth expectations of an enterprise may all reasonably explain a price drawdown. What is truly worth seeking is the divergence between stock price performance and fundamentals.

Related market research from Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley points out that earnings expectations for some enterprises are still improving, but stock prices have not yet reflected the changes in tandem. This may bring selective opportunities for industrials, manufacturing and other capital-intensive industries.

Therefore, judging whether US equities can usher in a broader rally depends not on how many stocks have already fallen 20%, but on whether the earnings of these enterprises can grow steadily, and whether financing conditions show substantive improvement.

## What Is the Biggest Risk for US Stocks in 2027?

Looking ahead to 2027, the main challenge facing US stocks is the dependence of index returns on the earnings and capital expenditure decisions of a small number of large companies.

If AI-related companies continue to deliver on growth expectations, while interest rates decline and earnings in other sectors improve, the market rally may gradually broaden. But if the return on AI investment falls short of expectations, or if energy and financing costs continue to weigh on corporate profits, a correction in a handful of high-weight stocks could quickly drag down the overall index.

Slickorps Ventures recommends closely watching the relative performance of the market-cap-weighted and equal-weighted S&P 500, the proportion of advancing stocks, earnings forecast revisions, and corporate free cash flow. Among these, the equal-weighted S&P 500 can reduce the influence of mega-caps on the index, offering a complementary perspective for judging the breadth of the market rally.

**The core contradiction in current US stocks is the coexistence of prosperity at the index level and deep divergence among constituent stocks.** This structure may both conceal investment opportunities where earnings improvements have not yet been fully reflected, and mean that the overall index is increasingly dependent on a few leading companies.

For investors, what truly merits study is not which stocks have fallen the most, but which companies can continue to generate cash flow amid changing financing conditions and gradually repair their valuations.

## The key to determining the quality of the US stock rally in 2027 may no longer be simply whether the S&P 500 can hit a new all-time high, but whether the next round of growth can be driven jointly by more companies. Only when market returns are no longer highly dependent on a small number of companies can the foundation of the US stock rally truly become more solid.
