Data from Goldman Sachs Prime Services shows that its hedge fund clients recorded their largest net selling of U.S. equities last week since the “Liberation Day” shock week in April 2025, abruptly reversing three consecutive weeks of net buying. According to market summaries of Goldman Prime data, 9 out of 11 sectors were sold, with the most pronounced pressure seen in information technology, industrials, utilities, healthcare, and materials; single stocks accounted for approximately 53% of the selling, while Macro Products such as ETFs and index instruments accounted for around 47%. Slickorps Ventures believes this structure is more worthy of analysis than a simple conclusion that “hedge funds have turned bearish on U.S. equities.” When both single stocks and index instruments are reduced at the same time, it is more likely to indicate that the risk budget of the entire portfolio is being repriced.  

What has changed in the market may not be that investors have suddenly overturned their previous economic views, but that they are no longer willing to express those views with the same positions and leverage as the week before.

## This Looks More Like De-Risking Than a Unified Bearish Bet

Net selling alone cannot tell us exactly what capital has done. It may come from long position reductions, increased short positions, or simultaneous adjustments across different strategies.  

The more explanatory data at present is that Long Sales among Goldman Prime clients were approximately 1.8 times New Shorts. This indicates that the current shift was mainly driven by reductions in existing long positions, rather than the broad establishment of new short positions.  

Therefore, Slickorps Ventures is more inclined to define the current move as net de-risking. It occurred after the large-scale de-grossing seen this summer, but the two should not be conflated: de-grossing requires a decline in total Gross Exposure across both Long and Short positions, whereas net selling alone does not prove that overall leverage has fallen simultaneously.  

Notably, as of mid-August, this group of funds still maintained year-to-date returns of around 10%. In other words, profits remain, but the willingness to carry positions has diminished.

## AI Crowding and Quantitative Shocks Are Consuming Risk Capacity

The recent sharp volatility in AI, Momentum, and Systematic strategies provides important context for this risk reduction.  

In mid-August, some systematic long-short strategies suffered significant single-day losses, while a pure Momentum index at one point fell by more than 4%. At the same time, mega-cap technology and AI-related assets remained among the most crowded hedge fund positions.  

The asset disposal by Situational Awareness offers a more direct example: after a substantial drawdown, the fund sold most of its public equity portfolio to Citadel, which then quickly reduced the risk it had taken on through nearly 100 block trades.  

Slickorps Ventures observes that these events collectively remind the market that when volatility and asset correlations rise, the same investment portfolio consumes more VaR, margin, and risk limits. Even if funds still believe in the long-term fundamentals, they may be forced to reduce positions in seemingly unrelated sectors such as industrials, healthcare, or materials.  

The broader the selling, the less it necessarily means that bad news is widespread; it may also mean that shared risk constraints are spreading across assets.

## The Real Danger Lies in the Absence of New Buyers After the Selling

A large-scale sell-off can simply be a position reset. Slickorps Ventures is more focused on three follow-up variables: whether selling continues, whether the active Short Book expands significantly, and who absorbs the stocks being reduced.  

Current fundamentals still provide clear support. Goldman recently noted that U.S. equity earnings are spreading from AI into more sectors, with 9 sectors in the S&P 500 maintaining strong earnings performance.  

As a result, an important divergence is emerging in the market:  

Corporate earnings can continue to grow, while institutions can reduce risk at the same time.  

If pensions, mutual funds, retail investors, and long-term capital are able to absorb the selling pressure, this adjustment may ultimately amount to nothing more than a transfer of holdings; however, if long positions continue to be reduced, short positions expand, market breadth deteriorates, and long-term buying weakens at the same time, Portfolio De-risking may evolve into a negative feedback loop in which price declines drive further position reductions.  

Slickorps Ventures believes that a market top truly worth watching sometimes does not begin with bad news.  

The story may still be believed, but the marginal investor has already decided to carry a little less risk.
