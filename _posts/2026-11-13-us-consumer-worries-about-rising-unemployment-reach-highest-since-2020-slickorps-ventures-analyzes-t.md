In October 2026, pessimistic expectations of US consumers regarding the future job market continued to heat up. According to the Survey of Consumer Expectations published by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York on November 9, the average probability that consumers assign to the US unemployment rate being higher than its current level 12 months from now rose to 45.3%, the highest level since April 2020 and up about 11.3 percentage points from January 2025. More noteworthy is that the relevant expectation among consumers under 40 reached 48.9%, while households with annual income above USD 100,000 reached 51.5%. **Slickorps Ventures research notes that a structural change is emerging in the US job market: the current unemployment rate is relatively stable, yet the confidence of workers in future job opportunities continues to weaken.** This pressure may first affect household consumption and career choices, then gradually transmit to corporate hiring and economic growth.

## Why Have Consumer Worries About Rising Unemployment Reached a Post-Pandemic High?

New York Fed data show that in October 2026 the average probability consumers assigned to the unemployment rate rising over the next year reached 45.3%, an increase of 1.4 percentage points month on month. At the same time, the average probability of losing one job in the next 12 months stood at 13.9%, and the average probability of finding a new job after losing the current one was only 46.5%.

[Data Source: New York Fed October 2026 Survey of Consumer Expectations](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261109.html)

It should be made clear that 45.3% represents the subjective probability judgment of consumers regarding a rise in the overall US unemployment rate, and does not mean that 45.3% of Americans expect to lose their own jobs.

Looking at the demographic structure, the relevant expectation among consumers under 40 reached 48.9%, and the group with annual income above USD 100,000 reached 51.5%. The New York Fed also noted that this round of rising overall pessimistic expectations was mainly driven by respondents without a college degree.

**This shows that job anxiety is spreading across income and age groups.** Young workers worry about shrinking career opportunities, high-income households focus on job stability, and workers with lower levels of education may be more directly affected by changes in hiring demand.

## Why Is Employment Confidence Deteriorating Continuously While the Actual Unemployment Rate Remains Stable?

According to [the October 2026 employment report of the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_11062026.htm), nonfarm payrolls in the United States increased by 165,000 that month, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%. But job openings and hiring activity reveal another side: in September 2026, U.S. job openings stood at about 7.5 million, hires at about 5.4 million, and voluntary quits at about 3.4 million.

This is a typical low-hiring, low-layoff configuration. Firms are not cutting staff on a large scale, yet they are also unwilling to actively expand hiring. For workers who already have jobs, holding on to their current positions may be relatively easy; but for graduates, the unemployed, and those hoping to switch jobs, securing new opportunities may be more difficult.

[Research by the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/policy-hub-macroblog/2026/10/06/rising-labor-market-anxiety-and-declining-quits) points out that when employees worry it will be hard to find a new job after leaving, their willingness to quit voluntarily declines, and the replacement hiring demand of firms may also decrease accordingly.

## Therefore, a stable unemployment rate does not mean the job market has not deteriorated. What truly deserves attention is whether workers can find new jobs, and whether firms remain willing to create employment opportunities.

## How Does Employment Pessimism Affect Consumption and Economic Growth?

When households worry about future income interruption, even if current wages have not fallen, they may reduce non-essential spending in advance, increase savings, and postpone large purchases.

The October survey of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York shows that the median expected household income growth over the next year is 3.0%, while the median expected household spending growth reaches 5.3%; the average probability of being unable to make the minimum debt payment over the next three months rises to 13.8%.

This reflects a potential contradiction among income expectations, consumer demand, and household debt pressure. If more households adopt cautious spending strategies, discretionary consumer sectors such as retail, dining, and travel may be the first to be affected, and weakening corporate revenue expectations may further suppress hiring demand.

At the same time, artificial intelligence and automation are changing the demand structure for some entry-level positions, administrative support, and basic analytical work. Technological progress can raise production efficiency, but if the pace of new job creation is insufficient to absorb affected workers, short-term job transition pressure may increase further.

## What Does the 2027 US Job Market Really Need to Watch?

## Slickorps Ventures believes that the 45.3% expected rise in the unemployment rate does not mean the US is about to enter a recession, but it reflects that the confidence of workers in future economic opportunities is weakening.

Going forward, key focus areas include nonfarm payroll growth, job openings, hiring rates, voluntary quit rates, the number of long-term unemployed, and household debt delinquency. If pessimistic consumer expectations gradually translate into consumption contraction and reduced corporate hiring, the currently relatively stable unemployment rate may fail to fully reflect underlying economic pressure.

Over the long term, job restructuring brought about by artificial intelligence, corporate capital allocation, and changes in worker skill demand will also determine whether the US job market can create enough new job opportunities.

**The key to the next phase of the US job market is not only avoiding mass layoffs, but also restoring job mobility and the possibility of career development**. Only when workers regain confidence in seeking better jobs and companies are willing to continuously create new positions can the job market truly regain vitality.
